Reference integration · Solana

Build around a fixed budget and deadline.

Your application owns the work. Capture402 enforces who can move the payment, how much, and when.

One payer authorization funds a hold. The merchant can collect in parts before the stored capture deadline. At or after it, the payer can reclaim the unused hold without the merchant or facilitator.

Four roles, separate responsibilities.

Payer
Independently checks the network, program, merchant, mint, maximum, deadlines, sponsor and complete transaction message before signing authorization. Retains the original payment identity and consent; signs direct reclaim when eligible.
Merchant
Decides what to charge and signs each capture, unused-balance release or refund. The merchant can collect up to the remaining hold before the capture deadline. The protocol does not verify usage or service delivery.
Facilitator
Validates economic signatures, terms and account roles, adds the configured sponsor signature and relays each exact transaction once. It sponsors relayed fees and authorization rent in this profile. It cannot replace payer or merchant consent. Its private gateway needs authentication and admission controls.
Application
Quotes the work, binds it to the original payment, obtains explicit consent, preserves signed attempts and durable journals, and verifies finalized effects before delivery. It owns idempotency, interruption handling and artifact access. A successful HTTP status or wallet approval alone is insufficient proof.

Agree once. Keep those terms.

The maximum, merchant, mint and capture/refund deadlines are immutable. The authorization cannot be topped up, extended or reopened. New terms require a new payer authorization, never an automatic response to a timeout. Each merchant operation signs the expected held and captured-unrefunded balances; stale consent fails onchain.

Classic SPL Token assets only. Token-2022 and wrapped SOL are rejected. Amounts are integer base units; show the configured mint and decimals before consent. The report example uses a six-decimal test mint, not real USDC.

Local x402 extension. The reference uses x402 v2 HTTP headers with scheme="auth-capture" and extra.paymentFlow="escrow". Its SVM wire profile is a proposed local extension. It is not an upstream-approved SVM binding, a published drop-in SDK or a promise of arbitrary x402 interoperability.

The facilitator exposes GET /supported, POST /verify (read-only preflight) and POST /settle. Capture, release (void) and refund are signed settle payloads, not new endpoints. The resource flow settles authorization directly; it does not insert a separate HTTP /verify call. Direct reclaim is never relayed through /settle.

Choose by authority, timing and exit.

For a known charge paid now, consider an immediate payment. For repeated or streamed spending, compare a payment channel. Capture402 fits a fixed Solana amount and capture deadline with merchant-signed collections and payer-signed recovery of the unused hold. Base Commerce Payments offers similar authorization, partial capture, expiry and payer reclaim on Base. None of these payment mechanisms verifies that a service was delivered.

Examined 16 October 2026. These are the named implementations and source revisions below, not a claim about every channel, x402 binding or deployment.

Immediate x402 exact on SVM
The payer signs a transaction for the specified recipient and amount; a merchant or separate sponsor may pay its network fee. The scheme settles that payment rather than reserving a ceiling for later captures, so it specifies no unused-hold reclaim or closure path. It is simpler when no reservation is needed. Solana Foundation Payment Channels / MPP sessions
A payer escrows a ceiling; cumulative vouchers signed by the configured signer authorize spending, and settlement/distribution can pay in parts. A payer can sign a top-up or start forced close. Forced close starts a relative grace period; the payee may settle and seal during grace, and anyone may seal after it. Payer withdrawal of unused funds requires a sealed channel. Voucher expiry is per voucher, not an immutable absolute capture cutoff stored at channel open. Opening, settlement, distribution and closure use transactions; channel and vault rent can be recovered through its closure path. This is a real alternative for repeated spending and unused recovery. Capture402
One payer authorization fixes the maximum and capture deadline; the stored merchant signs each capture strictly before that deadline. The payer can sign direct reclaim of the remaining hold at or after it, without waiting for a merchant close or channel grace period. The merchant may release unused funds early; a later merchant-funded refund is separate. Each operation needs a transaction, although capture and release can be atomic. The payment identity account remains allocated in v1, so its rent is not recovered by a close instruction. Program source ↗
Base Commerce Payments
Its immutable payment terms include a maximum, authorization expiry and refund expiry. A designated operator can partially capture before authorization expiry or void unused escrow; the payer can reclaim the remaining authorization after expiry. It therefore shares the central authorize/capture/reclaim idea. The examined design uses Base contracts, modular token collectors, configurable fee bounds and EVM transaction gas rather than this Solana program's account model. Existing example · synthetic-demo-v1

One authorization. Explicit captures. One saved result.

This fixed-price reference filters a synthetic dataset and computes a synthetic aggregate. It does not meter arbitrary compute or demonstrate customer demand. The payment lab is a separate application and delivers no report.

100 test tokens authorized → capture 40 → capture 25 → release unused 35. At six decimals these are 100,000,000; 40,000,000; 25,000,000; and 35,000,000 base units. The charges are fixed, regardless of the selected region.

  1. Prepare and retain the original quote

    GET /resource returns 402 with PAYMENT-REQUIRED and no work. GET /api/report-session creates a private session capability. POST /api/orders, with the same session, configured Origin, idempotency key, payer and region request, retains one unsigned quote and manifest. Preserve private preparation before requesting a signature.

  2. Independently verify, then authorize once

    prepareReport recomputes the manifest. submitReport checks independent client policy, rebuilds the complete unsigned message and compares it before accessing the payer signer. Only an identical message is signed. One POST /resource?order=<id> carries the canonical request and original PAYMENT-SIGNATURE. The application permanently reserves the run before relay.

  3. Prove each payment before proceeding

    After proven authorization, create the dataset privately, then merchant-sign capture 40. After that proof, create the aggregate privately and merchant-sign capture 25. Then merchant-sign release of the unused 35 (type=void). Private artifact bytes and hashes precede capture consent. Requests cannot choose amounts, stages or expected balances.

  4. Persist both artifacts before delivery

    Every original signature must be finalized without error, with exact transaction/account correspondence, expected SPL token deltas and a payment snapshot fenced after its slot. Only after all four proofs and durable persistence of the complete immutable response can HTTP 200 return both artifacts. There is no per-stage delivery. PAYMENT-RESPONSE is the final release receipt; the body contains all four proof rows.

  5. Retrieve purchased bytes after a lost response

    retrieveReport uses authenticated GET /api/orders/<id>/result for the same order. readPaidReport checks the response and artifact hashes. Retrieval never signs, settles or resumes work. Incomplete retrieval returns 409 without artifacts; another paid POST is a conflict, not a retry strategy.

Unused return and refund are different.

Merchant release
A merchant-signed void returns the whole remaining hold to the payer. Capture can also explicitly release the remainder atomically. This returns unused escrow; it does not reverse a charge.
Payer reclaim
At or after the stored capture deadline, the payer signs a transaction returning the positive remaining hold. Already-captured funds are unaffected. The payer has no general unilateral early cancellation in this profile. Expiry moves no funds automatically.
Merchant-funded refund
Before the separate refund deadline, the merchant can sign a refund from its own token account, bounded by captured, unrefunded value. It needs merchant consent and liquidity. It neither spends the vault nor restores the spending budget. Refund is outside the first paid-report implementation.

If the application server is unavailable.

The browser lab prepares and submits reclaim through application routes. Turning off merchant actions does not demonstrate reclaim with the application server stopped.

The existing standalone payer tools build and send reclaim directly to the program, with no resource server or facilitator participation. Use the independently pinned deployment/network, original payment account, payer wallet, a usable canonical token account, configured trusted RPC and preserved reclaim journal. The payer needs SOL for its transaction fee. Check finalized payment and Clock state: the capture deadline must have arrived and a positive hold must remain.

The wallet-signed tool first creates an exact unsigned offer and review digest; after explicit review, the payer signs those bytes and submits once. Keep offer files, signed wires, journals, keys and provider configuration private. After an uncertain response, observe the original signature and retain the reservation. Missing history does not prove non-execution; never replace the blockhash, switch providers or pay again to bypass uncertainty.

Plan for costs and interruptions.

Each authorization, capture, release, refund and reclaim requires an onchain transaction, except capture-and-release can be atomic in one transaction. Network base and configured priority fees still apply, including fees for failed transactions. This profile charges zero protocol fees; that does not make the lifecycle free.

The payment identity account remains allocated permanently in v1, even after balances reach zero. There is no close/reset instruction to recover its rent. Authorization also needs the canonical vault account; account creation and rent funding must be accounted for explicitly. No token-price, savings, throughput or latency estimate is promised here.

The report MVP uses a designated resource writer, designated facilitator and trusted private durable local filesystem. It is not distributed exactly-once delivery. An interrupted, pending or UNKNOWN run can block all new paid-report economics indefinitely, including prepared orders after restart. There is no lock timeout or automatic recovery. Completed purchases remain readable.

Preserve original records, wires, signatures, locks and reservations. Do not clear state, repeat paid POSTs or issue compensating release/refund to make a demo complete. Isolate the report store, journals and coordination domain from the payment lab. Operator diagnosis and any later repair need their own reviewed scope.
